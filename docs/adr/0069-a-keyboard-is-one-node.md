# ADR 0069 — A keyboard is one node, and its keys are generated

Status: accepted, amended: a board a sequence carries has its caps carry with it, drawn in the shader ([ADR 0154](0154-a-keyboard-rides-its-board.md)). `modes/keyboard` exists: platforms carrying a `Keyboard` component get their top face tiled
with keycaps, held down by whoever is standing over them.

## Context

The first real game: a clone of Roblox's `+1 Speed Keyboard Escape`. Researched before designing, and the
research corrected four things that had been assumed:

- Roblox files it as an **incremental simulator**, not an obby. The number that grows is *speed*; the obstacle
  course is the container.
- The **clicks are the increment's readout**, not polish. Every step is +1 speed and every key landing fires a
  mechanical click. Asking whether to ship it silent was asking to ship the number without the feel.
- **Speed cuts both ways.** High speed launches you over whole sections and makes the character very hard to
  steer. That tension is the skill, and without it the game is a counter going up.
- **Wins are collected along the route** and spent on boosts; speed can also be trained idle on a treadmill.

Kodjo's decisions for the first slice: one stage plus the speed number, no rebirth; clone the speed curve for
now; **key-driven, not footfall-driven**; falling returns you to the stage start.

And the one that reshaped everything, after a wrong assumption of mine: **the player is a giant on a
normal-sized keyboard**. A cap is 0.6 m against a 2 m character — a stride covers a few of them — rather than a
platform-sized key you stand on one at a time. Caps are surface detail that reacts.

## Decision

### The component is data; the game draws

`Keyboard` carries `pitch`, `fill`, `height`, `glyphs` and `seed`, and does nothing. Every cap is generated by
`modes/keyboard`'s own code through the handles ADR 0066 opened: the scene to add to, a per-frame hook, and the
drawn rig parts.

Two consequences of the count, which is hundreds per board:

- **No per-cap colliders.** The collider is the platform box already authored. Which cap somebody is on is
  arithmetic on their position — cheap, exact, and it works for other players with nothing on the wire.
- **No per-cap nodes.** Hundreds of nodes per platform would be unauthorable and unloadable.

### Occupancy, not footfalls

The first version detected footfalls and bobbed a cap per step. Wrong: a standing player's keys popped back up
under them. **Every cap under a character's footprint is held down until they leave**, so standing still leaves
a print of your feet in the board. A 0.4 m capsule on a 0.6 m pitch holds one to four caps.

`hold()` then `update(dt)`, called by the game in that order rather than from a hook inside `drawKeycaps` —
frame hooks run in registration order, so a hook made at construction would always settle the caps before the
game had said who was standing where, pressing everything one frame late.

### The cap is generated geometry, not a model

Taper, a dished top, rounded corners via a superellipse, and a legend on the top only. All arithmetic, so it
stays parametric instead of baked into a file, and the asset pipeline stays out of it. A model would only be
needed for a *stylised* cap.

The legend sits on the top alone because the geometry's own UVs put the top face across a legend tile and send
every wall vertex to **one point** in a blank tile beside it. A box could not: one instanced mesh takes one
material, so the glyph appeared on all six faces.

### Instancing shares the upload, not the drawing

This was got wrong first and it cost real frame rate. 26 draw calls sounded cheap and hid the fact that every
cap is drawn in **three** passes — the shadow map, the GTAO prepass (`DEFAULT_POSTFX.ao` is true and the level
overrides nothing) and the colour pass. At 352 triangles a cap, a 364-cap level submitted **384k triangles a
frame**.

Three fixes, largest first:

1. **Caps do not cast shadows.** A whole pass through a 2048² PCF-soft map for the self-shadowing of 0.3 m bumps
   on a flat board. The board still casts. They still *receive*, which is the player's shadow across the keys
   and costs no extra pass.
2. **Tessellation cut**: 32 perimeter segments → 12, dish rings 4 → 2. 352 → **84** triangles a cap.
3. **`computeBoundingSphere()` per instanced mesh.** Three was culling against the *unit cap's* sphere at the
   mesh origin, so a board 40 m down the level was never skipped.

Together: **384k → 71k triangles a frame**, and the test now enforces ≤100 triangles a cap.

## Consequences

- A new game on port 5177, `pnpm dev:keyboard`, and a level with five boards and 422 caps.
- **Other players press keys too**, because their animator runs on this machine (ADR 0063) so where their feet
  are is known here. Nothing on the wire.
- 28 tests. The one that matters is the round trip — place every cap, stand on each, get that cap back — on
  flat, rotated and tilted boards, because placement and lookup are two copies of one rule and disagreeing by
  one cell presses the key beside your foot. Breaking the lookup by a cell fails it in four places.
- The debug pane starts **collapsed, with every folder collapsed**, its own included: fifty tunables covering a
  quarter of the screen is worse than one click.

### Two bugs worth recording

**The standing band was 2.5 m.** Chosen when a body's position was being passed, never tightened when it became
a foot — so anything falling within 2.5 m pressed every key beneath it all the way down. Now 0.35 m.

**The sole is not the part's origin.** `partsOf` gives part centres and a lower leg is 0.375 m, so its centre
sits ~0.19 m above the foot. With the band tightened that alone would have stopped a standing player pressing
anything.

**The legend was mirrored.** A player runs along +z with the camera behind, so the camera's right is world
**−x** and `u` has to fall as `x` rises. A test pins both axes now.

## Rejected

**A real QWERTY layout.** Kodjo's call: individual caps with letters scattered over them, so the layout is
deliberately meaningless.

**Generating the keys in the loader.** The editor does **not** draw edit-mode meshes from the level loader — it
builds its own per node so they can be clicked and gizmoed, and runs the loader only for markers, triggers and
physics. Keys generated there would appear when play-testing and not while authoring.

**Real child nodes per cap.** Visible in the editor, but a board is hundreds of them.

**One mesh with per-instance UVs into a glyph atlas.** Needs a shader patch to save 25 draw calls.

**A footfall event from `@mp/humanoid`.** Reached for out of habit and not needed: ADR 0063 already lets a game
replace the animator, and the occupancy model made the question moot anyway.

## Known open

- **No speed number, no click, no Wins.** This is the surface the game hangs off, not the game.
- **The editor shows a plain box.** Caps appear only in play.
- **Feet clip into the caps.** The player stands on the platform's face and the caps rise 0.3 m above it.
- Letters still appear on cap **sides** where two boards' caps are seen edge-on from below.
- Only **box** platforms tile.
